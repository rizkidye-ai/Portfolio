01

Keeping the numbers honest

Problem
A busy cafe handles cash, stock, and partner consignment every day. Without clear controls, small leaks go unnoticed.
What I did
Wrote and ran a written SOP for the owner, admin, and cashiers, with the same rules for everyone.

How the controls work

  • Weekly stock opname with split duties: the cashier counts, the admin enters, and nobody enters their own count.
  • Daily review of the previous night's cash close, refunds, receivables, and expenses, each backed by proof.
  • One person, one account, so every transaction traces to a name.
  • Cash differences above a set threshold need a written explanation, and recurring issues are escalated with dates and figures.
  • Business and personal money kept separate through scheduled, recorded owner withdrawals.
02

Cleaning up the shop's books

Problem
Monthly rent was missing from the records, one "Miscellaneous" category absorbed a large share of revenue, and cost of goods sold was calculated per order.
What I did
Recalculated cost of goods sold from stock opname, recorded rent across its three lease components, and split the Miscellaneous category into proper expense lines.
Result
Cost of goods sold now comes from stock counts, and expenses are split into clear lines.

03

Finding the slow days

Question
Why does revenue stall on some days, and what would bring customers in?
What I did
Analyzed 4,000+ transactions and customer habits, then launched PlayStation rentals (one unit, later two) with a usage SOP, and put courtesy, cleanliness, and customer comfort first.
Also built
A sales visualization tool and a PlayStation rental pricing calculator, both interactive HTML.

What the data showed

  • Traffic was weakest on Mondays and Wednesdays.
  • Average spend per transaction was low.
  • Many customers bought only a minimum-spend item.
